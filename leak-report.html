01

Two websites competing with each other

Critical Confirmed
The leak
.com and .co.uk carry near-identical content for the same business. They split his link authority, create duplicate content, and cannibalise each other's rankings — two weak signals where there should be one strong one.
Where to check
Google: site:theloftman.com then site:theloftman.co.uk — both are indexed
Search Console → add both as properties → compare impressions & the Links report
IONOS → confirm he owns both domains & where each points
The fix
Pick the canonical domain (.com holds the authority), 301-redirect the other domain-wide, port the sharper .co.uk title onto it, then resubmit the sitemap.
Marketing

All SEO authority pools into one domain — one strong contender instead of two also-rans.

Leads

Every ranking sends visitors to a single, maintained page built to convert.

Brand

One coherent presence, not two half-tended sites a customer could stumble between.

02

No presence in the Google map pack

Critical Confirmed
The leak
No Google Business Profile surfaced in search — so he's absent from the map results that sit above the normal links and capture most "near me" local demand. For a trade, this is usually the single biggest lead source, and it's free.
Where to check
Google & Google Maps: search loft boarding Devizes — is he in the 3-pin pack?
Maps: look for an unclaimed pin at his address
business.google.com → is a profile claimed & verified?
The fix
Claim or create the profile, verify it, then fully populate: categories, services, service-area towns, real job photos, hours, and weekly posts.
Marketing

Puts him in the most prominent slot in local search — prime, unpaid real estate.

Leads

Map listings drive calls and direction taps directly, often the top enquiry channel.

Brand

A complete, photo-rich, reviewed profile looks legit at the exact moment of choosing.

03

Inconsistent name, address & phone (NAP)

Critical Confirmed
The leak
His details don't match across the web — the site says postcode SN10 5HF, directories say SN10 5FQ, and the name appears as both "The Loft Man" and "The Loft Man (Wilts) Ltd". Google can't fully trust a business whose own details disagree.
Where to check
His site's footer & contact page · the GBP · Which? Trusted Trader · Yell / Cylex / UK Small Business Directory · Facebook "About". List each and spot the mismatches.
The fix
Decide the one true NAP, record it on a master sheet, then update every listing to match it character-for-character.
Marketing

Consistent NAP is a core local-ranking trust signal — matching details lift map position.

Leads

Customers reach the right phone and address — no misdials, no lost enquiries.

Brand

Precise, uniform details read as professional; conflicting ones read as sloppy.

04

A 4.92★ review asset sitting dormant

High Confirmed
The leak
53 reviews at 4.92★ exist — but appear to live on Which?/third-party widgets, not Google, and aren't growing. Review count, freshness and velocity are what feed both local rankings and buyer confidence; frozen reviews do neither.
Where to check
Confirm where the 53 reviews sit today (Which? / Trustindex vs Google)
GBP → how many Google reviews, and how recent is the newest?
His site → are reviews shown on-page at all?
The fix
Funnel every finished job to a Google review (follow-up text + a QR "leave a review" card), and surface fresh reviews on the site via a widget.
Marketing

Review quantity, recency and velocity are direct local-ranking factors.

Leads

Visible stars lift click-through and close hesitant buyers — social proof at the decision point.

Brand

A steady stream of recent 5★ reviews signals a business busy and trusted now.

05

Contact form is the only way to reach him

High Confirmed
The leak
The site's only call-to-action is a "contact us" form. Most trade enquiries are phone calls and most of this traffic is on a phone — so the impatient, ready-to-book visitor has no one-tap way to call and quietly bounces.
Where to check
Open both sites on a phone: is there a tap-to-call button? A sticky CTA? How many fields does the contact form ask for before you can send anything?
The fix
Add a sticky click-to-call and WhatsApp button on mobile, and cut the form to the essentials (postcode, job type, phone).
Marketing

A higher conversion rate makes every visit — paid or organic — worth more, lowering effective cost per lead.

Leads

Removing friction on the phone-call path directly increases enquiries from the same traffic.

Brand

"Easy to reach, quick to answer" fits the honest-local-tradesman promise; a wall of fields feels cold.

06

No measurement or conversion tracking

High Verify
The leak
There's likely no proper GA4, Search Console, call tracking or Ads conversion tracking — so nobody can see which channels actually produce enquiries, or measure the traffic decline he's worried about. Every other fix is flying blind without this.
Where to check
View-source for a GA4 / gtag tag
search.google.com/search-console → is the site verified?
Google Ads → is conversion tracking switched on? · Ask him if calls are tracked at all
The fix
Install GA4, verify Search Console, add call tracking and Ads conversion tracking — then baseline the numbers before changing anything else.
Marketing

The instrument panel for everything — you can't improve rankings or spend you can't measure.

Leads

Shows which channels bring real enquiries, so effort and budget follow the leads.

Brand

Turns his marketing into an accountable operation — evidence-led decisions, provable ROI.

07

Dated site, likely slow on mobile

High Verify
The leak
The site looks its age, and dated builds are usually slow and awkward on a phone. Speed and mobile-friendliness are ranking factors, and a clunky first impression makes a skilled 13-year craftsman look out of date before he's said a word.
Where to check
pagespeed.web.dev → run both URLs, note the mobile Core Web Vitals scores
Open the site on a mid-range phone — how does it load and scroll?
The fix
A modern, fast, mobile-first rebuild (Phase 2 of the roadmap) with one clear value proposition.
Marketing

Speed and mobile-friendliness are ranking signals — slow sites shed rankings and impatient users.

Leads

A faster, cleaner site lowers bounce and lifts the share of visitors who actually enquire.

Brand

The site is often the first impression now, not the work — it should look as sharp as his finish.

08

Trust signals buried below the fold

Moderate Confirmed
The leak
His hard-won proof — the Which? Trusted Trader badge, the star rating, "13+ years · fully insured · guaranteed" — isn't in the hero. Visitors decide in seconds whether to trust a trade, and right now they have to scroll to find the reasons.
Where to check
Load the homepage on mobile and desktop: without scrolling, can you see the Which? badge, the rating, and the guarantees?
The fix
Hoist the badge, star rating and key guarantees into the above-the-fold hero.
Marketing

A stronger first impression cuts bounce — a modest but real engagement gain.

Leads

Trust shown before the scroll keeps more visitors on the page and moving toward an enquiry.

Brand

Leads with his credibility instead of hiding it — the badge is worth nothing unseen.

09

No structured-data (schema) markup

Moderate Confirmed
The leak
The pages have no schema markup, so Google can't pull his rating, services or FAQs into the search result itself. He's leaving free, attention-grabbing space in the listing unused.
Where to check
search.google.com/test/rich-results → run his URLs
View-source and search for application/ld+json — likely nothing there
The fix
Add LocalBusiness, Service, AggregateRating and FAQ schema across the site.
Marketing

Unlocks rich results (stars, FAQs) — a more prominent, higher click-through listing.

Leads

A result showing 4.92★ and answers pulls more clicks from the very same ranking.

Brand

A polished, information-rich listing looks more credible than a plain blue link.

10

Thin, duplicated on-page SEO

Moderate Confirmed
The leak
Page titles and descriptions are generic and — across the two domains — duplicated. They don't target how people actually search ("loft boarding Devizes", "loft ladder fitted Wiltshire"), so he ranks for far less than he could.
Where to check
View-source each page's <title> and meta description, or read them in Search Console → Pages. Compare the two domains for identical text.
The fix
Write a unique, keyword-led title, meta description and single H1 for every page on the canonical site.
Marketing

Titles that name the service and town lift rankings for the searches that actually buy.

Leads

Clear, specific titles and descriptions earn more clicks from the results page.

Brand

Precise page titles read as a business that knows exactly what it does and where.

11

Brand-name confusion with look-alikes

Moderate Confirmed
The leak
Similarly named traders (e.g. "The Loft Ladder Man") show up around his brand searches. Anyone specifically looking for him can land on a competitor instead — leaked traffic he already earned by reputation.
Where to check
Google his brand terms: the loft man devizes, the loft man wiltshire — see who else appears and whether his own profile dominates.
The fix
Own his branded search: a strong verified GBP, consistent naming everywhere, branded content, and secured social handles.
Marketing

Owning branded search protects the traffic of people already hunting for him by name.

Leads

Stops a ready-to-buy customer accidentally handing the job to a look-alike.

Brand

Cements "The Loft Man" as the recognised name across his patch of Wiltshire.

How to read this alongside the roadmap

These are the bleeds — things actively costing him now — so they map onto Phases 0–2 of the growth roadmap, front-loaded on purpose. Fix the three critical leaks first (they're cheap and fast), stand up measurement so you can prove the rest, then work down the list. The "verify" items just need a look once delegated account access is in place — don't guess, confirm.
